# The ending parchment in British English (a fresh translation of the tape's Spanish text; see pergamino_intro_en.txt).
# Paragraphs are separated by a blank line (the tape starts each new paragraph on the next line, indented by a space).
Dis~fig~ured by an~guish, by the tor~ment of the poi~son that was al~read~y wind~ing in plen~ty through his veins, the once ven~er~a~ble face of the old man look~ed re~pul~sive and gro~tesque.
We could have caught him calm~ly, but we threw our~selves up~on him with ve~he~mence. He man~aged to break free and pressed the book to his chest to de~fend it. I held him with my left hand, while with my right I tried to keep the lamp raised. But I brushed his face with the flame, and he let out a sti~fled sound, al~most a roar, let~ting scraps of pa~per fall from his mouth. His right hand let go of the book, searched for the lamp and, with one blow, tore it from me, hurl~ing it for~ward...
The oil spilled, and at once the fire caught the parch~ment, which blazed like a bun~dle of dry brush~wood. It all hap~pened in a few mo~ments; a great flame rose from the books, as if those age~old pages had spent cen~tur~ies wait~ing to burn and re~joiced to sat~is~fy at last an im~me~mo~ri~al thirst for ec~py~ro~sis.
The ab~bey burned for three days and three nights and the last ef~forts were all in vain. On the third day, once the wound~ed were tend~ed and the bod~ies that had been left out~side the build~ings bur~ied, the monks and the rest of the peo~ple of the ab~bey gath~ered their be~long~ings and left the pla~teau, which was still smok~ing, like a cursed place.
Wil~liam and I went a~way from that place on two mounts that we found in the for~est. When we reached Mu~nich I had to part, not with~out shed~ding a~bun~dant tears, from my good mas~ter. Af~ter giv~ing me much good ad~vice for my fu~ture stud~ies, he em~braced me, with the ten~der~ness of a fa~ther, and bade me fare~well. I nev~er saw him a~gain.
Today, on the thresh~old of death, the more I reread the sto~ry that has come of it, the less I know whether it holds a plot that can be told a~part from the mere nat~u~ral suc~ces~sion of events and of the mo~ments that link them to one an~oth~er. And it is hard for this old monk not to know whether the let~ter he has writ~ten holds an~y hid~den mean~ing, or whether it holds more than one, or ma~ny, or none.
But per~haps this in~ca~pa~ci~ty is the pro~duct of the shad~ow that the great dark~ness now draw~ing near casts up~on this world grown old.
It is cold in the scrip~to~ri~um, my thumb aches. I leave this text, I know not for whom, this text, of which I no long~er know what it speaks.
