# The Commodore P500

[Home](Home.md) · previous: [The Commodore 128](C128.md) · next: [Loading: tape and disks](Loading.md)

The P500 is the CBM-II 510: a MOS 6509, the C64's VIC-II and SID, 128 KiB of RAM, IEEE-488 disk drives.  abadia128ks
runs on it as the C128's full game (`-D C128`: the second bank's data, the high-resolution heads, the Ave María) on a
platform of its own (`-D P500`: `src/c64/plataforma/p500.s`, `interrupcion500.s`, `teclado500.s`, `tinta500.s`,
`io500.inc`).  Every machine fact used comes from Commodore's 700 Reference Guide, the KERNAL ROM 901234-02 and cc65's
cbm510 runtime, and was checked in VICE's xcbm5x0 (`doc/referencias/p500.md`).

## The 6509's banks

The 6509 addresses 16 banks of 64 KiB.  It has no instruction of its own: two registers at `$0000` and `$0001` of every
bank choose the bank the program runs in and the bank of the only two cross-bank instructions, `LDA (zp),Y` and
`STA (zp),Y`.  The game runs in bank 0 with `$01 = 0`; its rooms, texts, the heads' black strokes and the Ave María
are in bank 1; every chip is in bank 15:

{{code src/c64/plataforma/io500.inc | ^; The MOS 6509 has the 6502's instructions | +5}}

What the C128 reaches by mapping block 1 with its MMU, the P500 reaches one byte at a time through the indirect bank
register; `far_lee` is the room table's reader:

{{code src/c64/plataforma/p500.s | ^\.proc far_lee | ^\.endproc}}

A plain `LDA abs` always reads the bank the program runs in: the copy of a head's black strokes from bank 1 had to
become an indirect read too (`cabezas128.s`, `neg_b1_copia`).

## The 300 Hz interrupt

The game's tick comes from the 6526's timer A, through the first 6525 (TPI 1), the CBM-II's interrupt controller.  The
order of the acknowledgement matters: TPI 1 ended while the 6526's line is still down makes the interrupt come again
at once, for ever; the 6526 read only before TPI 1 loses a tick that falls between the two, and then no tick comes any
more.  So the 6526, TPI 1, and the 6526 again:

## The keyboard

The CBM-II keyboard is a matrix of 16 columns and 6 rows on the second 6525 (TPI 2), read as the KERNAL reads it (each
row twice, until two reads agree), and the two game ports are on the 6526; the result is the CPC's matrix the game
reads, as on the C64:

## The KERNAL of bank 15

Saving and loading go through the KERNAL, which has its own zero page and stack in bank 15: nothing of the game's has
to be moved (the C128 swaps its RAM with a photograph).  The call between banks is the KERNAL's own protocol, the one
cc65's runtime uses too: bank 15's stack gets the way back, the registers and the routine's address; `STA $00` at
`$FFF6`, the same in both banks, moves the CPU to bank 15:

## Loading

The menu, the parchment and the loader are a cc65 program for the cbm510 target.  It loads the game's bank 1 and its
bank 0, crunched, into bank 1, and starts the last stage there: running in bank 1, it decrunches into bank 0 with the
two cross-bank instructions (Exomizer's decruncher unchanged: its literals are read in the bank that runs, its copies
and its output go through `LDA`/`STA (zp),Y`), loads the Ave María and enters the game:

{{code src/c64/discop500/etapa500.s | ^; the game: bank 0's \$FFF6-\$FFFA | ^salto: }}

The stage stays in bank 1 for the whole game: at 100 % the game jumps back to it, and it loads the ending over the
game and starts it as BASIC's `SYS` would (`src/c64/discop500/NOTAS.md`).

## Certified against the PC

The P500's game is certified frame by frame against the PC rebuild, as the C128's: four random sessions of 6000 frames
from its own disk, 237 data compared at every frame, 0 differences (see [Building and certification](Building.md)).
