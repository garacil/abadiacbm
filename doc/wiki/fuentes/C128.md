# The Commodore 128

[Home](Home.md) · previous: [The Commodore 64 platform](Platform-C64.md) · next: [The Commodore P500](P500.md)

abadia128ks runs in the C128's own mode, not in its C64 mode.  It is the same 6502 game, assembled with `-D C128`
(the machine), `-D COMPLETO` (the full game of the CPC 6128 and PC versions) and `-D VELOZ` (the speed work), and it
uses what the C128 adds: a second 64 KiB of RAM, 2 MHz, and room for the high-resolution heads.  Every C128 fact used
was read in *Mapping the Commodore 128* (Ottis R. Cowper, COMPUTE! Books, 1986; `doc/referencias/c128.md`) and checked
in VICE's x128.

## Two blocks of RAM

The game runs in block 0, as on the C64; the MMU's configuration register `$FF00` takes the place of the C64's
`$01`.  Block 1 holds what the map generator moved out of block 0: the room table, the texts, the heads' black
strokes.  The common RAM at the bottom (4 KiB, always block 0) holds the routines that reach block 1 and the interrupt
handlers, so that an interrupt can come whichever block is mapped:

{{code src/c64/plataforma/c128.s | ^; abadia64ks, the Commodore 128's own platform | ^; SALA: 256 bytes}}

The game reads the room table through two macros (`LEE_SALA`, `PON_SALA`, see [From the Z80 to the 6502](From-Z80-to-6502.md))
that become `JSR far_lee` / `JSR far_pon` on the C128, and a room record is copied whole into the common RAM before it
is built.

## 2 MHz in the borders

The 8502 can run at 2 MHz, but the VIC-II cannot show a picture then.  The game switches to 2 MHz while the beam is

## The high-resolution heads

The multicolour bitmap halves the horizontal resolution, and the characters' faces lose their one-pixel features.  On
the C128 every head — Guillermo's, Adso's, every monk's face, and their reflections in the mirror — gets its black
strokes from a **hires hardware sprite** of the VIC-II, at the PC's full resolution, laid over the multicolour head that
keeps the fills.  The sprite and the bitmap must change in the same displayed frame, and a tile in front of a head must
hide its sprite's pixels too:

## The Ave María

At completas the PC version played a digitised Ave María.  The C128 plays the same samples (8062 a second, from the PC
version's data) through the SID's volume register, with the game frozen while it sounds, as on the PC:

{{code src/c64/plataforma/c128.s | ^; c128_ave: the game frozen | +7}}

The start menu's option 4 turns it off: the loader replaces its call by three `NOP`s and the samples are not even
loaded.

## The ending's load

At 100 % the game loads the ending program ("FINAL") with the KERNAL.  The game had overwritten the KERNAL's interrupt
code and vectors in RAM (`$FF05-$FF44`, `$FFFA-$FFFF`) in both blocks; with a 1541 the slow load takes interrupts
between bytes, which jumped into the game's code.  So `plat_final` first copies them back from the ROM into the RAM of
both blocks, as the KERNAL's own reset does, and the ending loads with a 1541 or a 1571:

{{code src/c64/plataforma/c128.s | ^        ldy     #\$40                    ; the KERNAL's interrupt | ^        bcs     kram}}
