# The Commodore P500

[Home](Home.md) · previous: [The Commodore 128](C128.md) · next: [Loading: tape and disks](Loading.md)

The P500 is the CBM-II 510: a MOS 6509, the C64's VIC-II and SID, 128 KiB of RAM, IEEE-488 disk drives.  abadia128ks
runs on it as the C128's full game (`-D C128`: the second bank's data, the high-resolution heads, the Ave María) on a
platform of its own (`-D P500`: `src/c64/plataforma/p500.s`, `interrupcion500.s`, `teclado500.s`, `tinta500.s`,
`io500.inc`).  Every machine fact used comes from Commodore's 700 Reference Guide, the KERNAL ROM 901234-02 and cc65's
cbm510 runtime, and was checked in VICE's xcbm5x0 (`doc/referencias/p500.md`).

## The 6509's banks

The 6509 addresses 16 banks of 64 KiB.  It has no instruction of its own: two registers at `$0000` and `$0001` of every
bank choose the bank the program runs in and the bank of the only two cross-bank instructions, `LDA (zp),Y` and
`STA (zp),Y`.  The game runs in bank 0 with `$01 = 0`; its rooms, texts, the heads' black strokes and the Ave María
are in bank 1; every chip is in bank 15:

[`src/c64/plataforma/io500.inc`](../../src/c64/plataforma/io500.inc#L20-L24), lines 20–24:

```asm
; The MOS 6509 has the 6502's instructions and no others; what it adds is two registers at $0000 and $0001 of every
; bank: the execution bank (where the program runs) and the indirect bank, used by two instructions only, LDA (zp),Y
; and STA (zp),Y, which read or write that bank (Commodore's 700 reference manual; cc65's cbm510 runtime works so).
; The game runs in bank 0 with $01 = 0, so its own (zp),Y accesses stay in bank 0; an I/O register is reached by
; $01 = 15, LDA/STA (pointer),Y, $01 = 0.
```

What the C128 reaches by mapping block 1 with its MMU, the P500 reaches one byte at a time through the indirect bank
register; `far_lee` is the room table's reader:

[`src/c64/plataforma/p500.s`](../../src/c64/plataforma/p500.s#L113-L119), lines 113–119:

```asm
.proc far_lee
        inc     P5_IND                  ; bank 1
        lda     (zhl),y
        dec     P5_IND                  ; bank 0
        ora     #0                      ; (N and Z of A)
        rts
.endproc
```

A plain `LDA abs` always reads the bank the program runs in: the copy of a head's black strokes from bank 1 had to
become an indirect read too (`cabezas128.s`, `neg_b1_copia`).

## The chips of bank 15

The code shared with the C64 and the C128 writes the VIC-II and the SID through three macros: on those machines each
is the plain instruction, the same bytes as before; on the P500 a short call that puts `$01 = 15` around one
`STA (zp),Y`:

[`src/c64/plataforma/io500.inc`](../../src/c64/plataforma/io500.inc#L66-L73), lines 66–73:

```asm
.macro P5_STA rutina, reg               ; (the P500's STA: A to the register through the routine)
        php
        sei                             ; (before P5_A: an interrupt's own macros use it)
        sta     P5_A
        lda     #reg
        jsr     rutina
        plp
.endmacro
```

[`src/c64/plataforma/p500.s`](../../src/c64/plataforma/p500.s#L249-L261), lines 249–261:

```asm
p5_vic_k:
        sty     P5_Y
        tay
vic_cola:
        lda     #P5_SISTEMA
        sta     P5_IND
        lda     P5_A
        sta     (P5_PVIC),y
        lda     #0
        sta     P5_IND
        lda     P5_A
        ldy     P5_Y
        rts
```

## The 300 Hz interrupt

The game's tick comes from the 6526's timer A, through the first 6525 (TPI 1), the CBM-II's interrupt controller.  The
order of the acknowledgement matters: TPI 1 ended while the 6526's line is still down makes the interrupt come again
at once, for ever; the 6526 read only before TPI 1 loses a tick that falls between the two, and then no tick comes any
more.  So the 6526, TPI 1, and the 6526 again:

[`src/c64/plataforma/interrupcion500.s`](../../src/c64/plataforma/interrupcion500.s#L176-L222), lines 176–222:

```asm
; the game's build: the game's sys_tick (2899h) inline, as interrupcion.s's: the 6526 and the 6525 acknowledged, the
; tick counter [289C] + 1 kept at FFh, the sound (0CD0: the opcode at plat_irq_sonido, JSR while a channel plays, BIT
; else) and the phrase scroller (368A).  t_frase lets a further tick in (its CLI): this handler is re-entrant.
.proc plat_irq
        pha
        tya
        pha
        lda     P5_IND
        pha
        lda     P5_PIO
        pha
        lda     P5_PIO+1
        pha
        lda     #P5_SISTEMA
        sta     P5_IND
        ldy     #$0D                    ; the 6526's flags read: its line up
        lda     (P5_PCIA),y
        ldy     #$07                    ; TPI 1's active interrupt ended (written back); with the 6526's line still
        lda     (P5_PTPI1),y            ;   down it would be taken again at once, an endless stream of interrupts
        sta     (P5_PTPI1),y
        ldy     #$0D                    ; the 6526's flags again: an underflow between the two reads, whose interrupt
        lda     (P5_PCIA),y             ;   TPI 1 has just ended, would leave its line down for ever (no new edge, no
                                        ;   tick any more); this read puts it up (that one tick is lost)
        lda     #0
        sta     P5_IND                  ; the game's data
        cld
        inc     D64_SYS_TICKS           ; 2899h: the tick counter, kept at FFh
        bne     :+
        dec     D64_SYS_TICKS
:
::plat_irq_sonido:
        bit     s_interrupcion          ; 0CD0: JSR ($20) while a channel plays (objetos_sonido.s), BIT ($2C) else
        inc     D64_TXT_SCROLL_TIMER    ; 368A's test: the scroller's 2Dh-th tick
        lda     D64_TXT_SCROLL_TIMER
        cmp     #$2D
        bne     :+
        jsr     t_frase                 ; (X and Y kept)
:       pla
        sta     P5_PIO+1
        pla
        sta     P5_PIO
        pla
        sta     P5_IND
        pla
        tay
        pla
        rti
```

## The keyboard

The CBM-II keyboard is a matrix of 16 columns and 6 rows on the second 6525 (TPI 2), read as the KERNAL reads it (each
row twice, until two reads agree), and the two game ports are on the 6526; the result is the CPC's matrix the game
reads, as on the C64:

[`src/c64/plataforma/teclado500.s`](../../src/c64/plataforma/teclado500.s#L146-L172), lines 146–172:

```asm
columna:
        ldy     #0
        lda     col_pa,x
        sta     (P5_PIO),y
        iny
        lda     col_pb,x
        sta     (P5_PIO),y
        iny
:       lda     (P5_PIO),y              ; the rows (until two reads agree: LDA (zp),Y twice, the 6509's CMP (zp),Y
        sta     filas_ant               ;   would read bank 0)
        lda     (P5_PIO),y
        cmp     filas_ant
        bne     :-
        ora     #$C0                    ; (bits 6-7: the VIC-II's bank lines)
        eor     #$FF
        beq     siguiente
        sta     pulsadas                ; keys down in column x
        ldy     seis,x                  ; 6 x + row
tecla:  lsr     pulsadas
        bcc     :+
        jsr     aplica
:       iny
        lda     pulsadas
        bne     tecla
siguiente:
        dex
        bpl     columna
```

## The KERNAL of bank 15

Saving and loading go through the KERNAL, which has its own zero page and stack in bank 15: nothing of the game's has
to be moved (the C128 swaps its RAM with a photograph).  The call between banks is the KERNAL's own protocol, the one
cc65's runtime uses too: bank 15's stack gets the way back, the registers and the routine's address; `STA $00` at
`$FFF6`, the same in both banks, moves the CPU to bank 15:

[`src/c64/plataforma/p500.s`](../../src/c64/plataforma/p500.s#L375-L435), lines 375–435:

```asm
.proc k15
        php
        pha
        lda     $01FF                   ; (the bottom of the game's stack, back after the call)
        sta     fondo
        lda     #P5_SISTEMA
        sta     P5_IND
        txa
        pha
        tya
        pha
        sei
        ldy     #$FF
        lda     (K_P1),y                ; bank 15's stack pointer
        tay
        lda     #0                      ; the bank to come back to
        sta     (K_P1),y
        dey
        lda     #>(K_EXCRTS - 1)
        sta     (K_P1),y
        dey
        lda     #<(K_EXCRTS - 1)
        sta     (K_P1),y
        tya
        sec
        sbc     #7
        sta     k_sp
        tay
        pla                             ; Y, X, A, P
        iny
        sta     (K_P1),y
        pla
        iny
        sta     (K_P1),y
        pla
        iny
        sta     (K_P1),y
        pla
        iny
        sta     (K_P1),y
        lda     k_dir                   ; the routine's address - 1
        iny
        sta     (K_P1),y
        lda     k_dir+1
        iny
        sta     (K_P1),y
        ldy     k_sp
        lda     #>(K_EXPULL - 1)
        sta     (K_P1),y
        dey
        lda     #<(K_EXPULL - 1)
        sta     (K_P1),y
        dey
        tsx
        stx     $01FF                   ; bank 0's stack pointer, for the way back ($FF11)
        tya
        tax
        txs
        lda     #P5_SISTEMA
        jmp     K_CAMBIA
.endproc
```

## Loading

The menu, the parchment and the loader are a cc65 program for the cbm510 target.  It loads the game's bank 1 and its
bank 0, crunched, into bank 1, and starts the last stage there: running in bank 1, it decrunches into bank 0 with the
two cross-bank instructions (Exomizer's decruncher unchanged: its literals are read in the bank that runs, its copies
and its output go through `LDA`/`STA (zp),Y`), loads the Ave María and enters the game:

[`src/c64/discop500/etapa500.s`](../../src/c64/discop500/etapa500.s#L129-L147), lines 129–147:

```asm
; the game: bank 0's $FFF6-$FFFA = STA $00, JMP principal_inicio; the switch from bank 1's $FFF6
juego:  lda     #0
        sta     IND
        lda     #<$FFF6
        sta     PTR
        lda     #>$FFF6
        sta     PTR + 1
        ldy     #4
:       lda     salto,y
        sta     (PTR),y
        dey
        bpl     :-
        lda     #$85                    ; bank 1's $FFF6: STA $00
        sta     K_CAMBIA
        lda     #$00
        sta     K_CAMBIA + 1
        lda     #0                      ; bank 0
        jmp     K_CAMBIA
salto:  .byte   $85, $00, $4C, <J_PRINCIPAL_INICIO, >J_PRINCIPAL_INICIO
```

The stage stays in bank 1 for the whole game: at 100 % the game jumps back to it, and it loads the ending over the
game and starts it as BASIC's `SYS` would (`src/c64/discop500/NOTAS.md`).

## Certified against the PC

The P500's game is certified frame by frame against the PC rebuild, as the C128's: four random sessions of 6000 frames
from its own disk, 237 data compared at every frame, 0 differences (see [Building and certification](Building.md)).
